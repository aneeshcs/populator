A Diagnostic Comparison against CESM--LENS POP2}
\author{POP Ocean Emulator project}

\begin{abstract}
We diagnose the leading modes of ocean variability reproduced by the
physics--informed ocean emulator and compare them against the parent POP2 model
(CESM Large Ensemble, member 001, held out from training). The emulator is
integrated as a free--running, surface--forced rollout from a single POP initial
condition; at every month it is supplied the same surface fluxes
(\texttt{SHF}, \texttt{SHF\_QSW}, \texttt{SFWF}, \texttt{TAUX}, \texttt{TAUY})
that drive POP, and it predicts its own ocean state forward. Over the window in
which the rollout remains physically stable (\textbf{67 months $\approx$ 5.6
years}), the emulator reproduces the El Ni\~no--Southern Oscillation (ENSO)
Ni\~no~3.4 index with correlation $r=0.77$, the North Atlantic SST index with
$r=0.68$, and the leading North Pacific SST principal component with $r=0.67$.
The spatial pattern of SST variability is captured in its broad geography, but
the emulator over--concentrates North Pacific variance into a single, overly
broad mode, and the free rollout drifts and becomes unstable beyond
$\sim$5.6~years. These results establish that the emulator has learned
physically meaningful interannual variability while highlighting long--horizon
rollout stability as the key remaining challenge.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}
The trained emulator (run~2 checkpoint, 188.9\,M parameters) is initialised from
the POP state of member~001 at model month $t_0=360$ and stepped forward one
month at a time. Each step consumes the POP surface forcing for that month and
the emulator's own previous state, so the comparison isolates how faithfully the
learned dynamics reproduce the \emph{ocean response} to a common forcing.

Monthly sea surface temperature (SST, the model's top--level \texttt{TEMP}) and
sea surface height (\texttt{SSH}) are collected for both the emulator and POP
over the same months. Anomalies are formed by removing the monthly climatology
(per calendar month) computed over the rollout window; indices are additionally
linearly detrended. All spatial averages are area--weighted by the POP T--cell
area \texttt{TAREA} and masked to ocean points. The leading empirical orthogonal
function (EOF) is obtained from an area--weighted singular value decomposition of
the anomaly matrix, and its spatial pattern is shown as the regression of SST
anomalies onto the standardised first principal component (PC1).

\paragraph{Rollout stability.} The free--running rollout was requested for 480
months (40 years) but became numerically unstable after \textbf{67 months}; all
diagnostics below therefore use the 5.6--year stable segment. This record length
is adequate to characterise interannual (ENSO) variability and to display the
geography of the leading modes, but it is too short for a robust assessment of
genuinely decadal--to--multidecadal behaviour. We report the Pacific and Atlantic
patterns as the \emph{leading SST modes over the stable window} rather than as
fully resolved decadal oscillations, and we treat the 5.6--year stability limit
itself as a primary diagnostic finding (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{El Ni\~no--Southern Oscillation (Ni\~no 3.4)}
The Ni\~no~3.4 index is the SST anomaly area--averaged over
\SIrange{5}{5}{\degree} latitude (\ang{5}S--\ang{5}N) and
\SIrange{170}{120}{\degree}W, the canonical ENSO monitoring region.
Figure~\ref{fig:nino} shows that the emulator (red) tracks the POP index (black)
closely throughout the stable window, reproducing the warm events near rollout
years~0.5 and~1.5 and the sustained cold phase around years~2.5--3.0. The
correlation is $r=0.77$ and the emulator's variance is 88\% of POP's, i.e.\ it
slightly underestimates ENSO amplitude but captures its phase and the
$\sim$2--4~year recurrence well. ENSO is the most robustly resolved mode here
because its timescale is fully contained within the 5.6--year record.

\section{Pacific Decadal Oscillation (leading N.\ Pacific SST mode)}
The PDO is defined as the leading EOF of North Pacific
(\ang{20}--\ang{70}N, \ang{110}E--\ang{100}W) monthly SST anomalies after
removal of the global--mean SST anomaly. Figure~\ref{fig:pdo} compares the EOF1
spatial patterns and Figure~\ref{fig:pdopc} the principal components.

POP exhibits the textbook PDO ``horseshoe'': a cool anomaly in the central/western
basin near \ang{40}N, \ang{160}E surrounded by warm anomalies along the eastern
boundary, explaining 28\% of the regional variance. The emulator reproduces the
\emph{timing} of the leading mode (PC1 correlation $r=0.67$,
Fig.~\ref{fig:pdopc}) but its spatial pattern is much broader and more
monopole--like, and its EOF1 accounts for 75\% of the variance---far more than
POP's 28\%. In other words, the emulator concentrates North Pacific variability
into a single, spatially smooth basin--scale mode rather than POP's richer
dipolar structure. This over--smoothing and over--dominance is consistent with
(i) the short 5.6--year record, in which a basin--wide drift component projects
strongly onto EOF1, and (ii) the diffusive tendency of the convolutional
emulator to damp fine--scale gradients.

\section{North Atlantic SST mode (AMO--like)}
The Atlantic index is the SST anomaly averaged over the North Atlantic
(\ang{0}--\ang{60}N, \ang{80}W--\ang{0}) with the global--mean SST anomaly
removed, the standard construction for the Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation
(AMO). Over a 5.6--year window this index measures interannual rather than
multidecadal variability, but it remains a useful test of basin--scale Atlantic
SST response. Figure~\ref{fig:amo} shows good agreement ($r=0.68$, variance ratio
0.93): the emulator captures the sign and amplitude of most fluctuations, with
occasional phase offsets of a few months.

\section{Geography of SST variability}
Figure~\ref{fig:sstvar} maps the standard deviation of monthly SST anomalies for
POP and the emulator, together with the time--mean SST bias. The emulator places
the maxima of SST variability in the correct locations---the equatorial Pacific
cold tongue (ENSO), the western boundary currents (Kuroshio, Gulf Stream) and the
high--latitude frontal zones---confirming that it has learned \emph{where} the
ocean is most active. The emulator field is visibly smoother and somewhat noisier
in patches, and the mean SST bias (right panel) is generally small but shows
structure along the western boundary currents and sea--ice margins where
gradients are sharpest. (The cross--hatched artefacts at the northern edge are
the POP tripole grid fold rendered on a regular longitude--latitude mesh, not a
model feature.)

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
Two messages emerge. First, \textbf{the emulator has learned physically
meaningful variability}: within its stable window it reproduces ENSO with
$r=0.77$, the leading North Pacific and North Atlantic SST modes with
$r\approx0.67$--$0.68$, and the correct geography of SST variance, all while
conserving heat and salt to within $\sim$0.1\% over the rollout. This is a
substantial advance over the project's first training run, whose forecasts were
statistically indistinguishable from persistence.

Second, \textbf{long--horizon rollout stability is the principal open problem}.
The free--running integration drifts and becomes unstable after $\sim$5.6~years,
which both limits the present diagnostics to interannual timescales and signals
error accumulation under autoregressive feedback. The over--dominant, overly
smooth PDO pattern is a symptom of the same issue: a slowly growing basin--scale
drift projects onto---and inflates---the leading EOF.

The most promising remedies, in order, are: (i)~\emph{longer multi--step rollout
training}: the present model reached only 4--step rollout in its final hours, and
extending the curriculum (8--12 steps) directly penalises the error growth that
destabilises long rollouts; (ii)~\emph{training on anomalies relative to a
monthly climatology}, which removes the large persistent mean state that the
drift rides on; (iii)~\emph{more training data} (additional CESM--LENS members,
including RCP8.5) for a broader sampling of variability; and (iv)~light
spectral or gradient regularisation to counter the convolutional smoothing that
weakens sharp SST fronts. Each of these is a configuration change in the existing
pipeline. Once a multi--decade rollout is stable, the same diagnostics can be
re--run to assess genuinely decadal modes (PDO, AMO) on their native timescales.
